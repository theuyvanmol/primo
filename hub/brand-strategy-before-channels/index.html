Brand strategy before channels: a practical starting point

Before deciding how often to post or which platform to advertise on, make a few choices about who you want to reach and why they should care.

Start with the decision you want to influence

A marketing brief often begins with a channel: more Instagram content, a Google campaign or a new website. Those can be useful tools, but the first question is what needs to change for the business. Do people need to discover the brand, understand a new offer, compare services or return more often? Choose one main decision to influence. A clear objective makes it easier to judge which activity belongs in the plan and which can wait.

Describe the audience in a useful way

An audience description should help the team make creative decisions. Age and location may matter, but the situation matters too. A parent looking for learning support has different questions from a visitor choosing a restaurant for a special occasion. Write down what the person is trying to achieve, what may hold them back and what information would help. This gives a designer, writer or media planner something more useful than a broad demographic label.

Make the offer easy to explain

Try explaining the offer without using a slogan. State what it is, who it helps and the next step. If the explanation changes depending on who is speaking, the marketing may need a clearer message before it needs more reach. This does not remove personality. It gives the personality a job: making a useful, distinctive offer easier to recognise and remember.

Choose proof you can actually support

A strong message needs a reason to believe it. That might be a real product detail, an approved customer story, a clear explanation of the process or a useful demonstration. Avoid building the campaign around claims the team cannot substantiate. Decide which evidence is available now and which would need approval. This makes content production more focused and reduces the gap between what the campaign promises and what the customer experiences.

Let the channel follow the job

Once the audience, offer and next action are clear, compare the available channels. Search can be useful when people already know what they need. Social content can help introduce a perspective or demonstrate an experience. A website can organise the details that support a decision. The best combination depends on the journey, the available creative and the team’s ability to maintain it. A smaller, coherent plan is often easier to learn from than a presence everywhere.

Turn the strategy into a working brief

Finish with a one-page brief: the business objective, audience situation, main message, available proof, intended action and how the team will review progress. Use it to check the first round of content and campaign ideas. If an idea does not help the audience take the intended next step, refine it or leave it out. Strategy becomes useful when it changes what the team makes next.
